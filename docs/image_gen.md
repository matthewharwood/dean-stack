# Prompt Template: Hadal Tide Bestiary Plate

Create a single fantasy enemy illustration for the **Hadal Tide** bestiary of a purely and organically created drowned elemental spirit (an "echo" of magma, current, sand, pressure, or bioluminescence). This image should be authored from scratch, designed to feel like a premium hand-painted creature plate from a classic RPG art book: imaginative, elegant, dangerous, and clearly readable as a game enemy from a cozy-mythic deep-sea setting.

This should feel like a genuinely designed creature with its own anatomy, behavior, and threat identity, organically conceptualized for the game's world.

---

## Fixed Series Style Rules

These rules should remain consistent across the entire bestiary:

- **Format:** square
- **Subject count:** one creature only
- **Composition:** full body fully visible inside the frame
- **Framing:** no cropping, no bleed, no overlap outside the image
- **Background:** high-key, clean, airy, off-white, cream, or pale watercolor wash
- **Environment:** no scenic background, no landscape, no room interior, no floor plane unless extremely subtle
- **Presentation:** isolated bestiary plate / natural-history style creature illustration
- **Medium:** watercolor and colored pencil
- **Texture:** visible watercolor bloom, pigment granulation, subtle paper texture, fine colored-pencil edge work
- **Rendering:** painterly, refined, tactile, elegant, museum-quality fantasy illustration
- **Lighting:** soft, diffuse, tasteful illustration lighting
- **Mood:** mysterious, dangerous, strange, collectible, bestiary-worthy
- **Silhouette:** instantly readable
- **Negative space:** generous, airy, intentional
- **Detail handling:** use detail clustering and visual hierarchy, not evenly distributed detail
- **Design level:** premium concept art, not mascot art, not clip art, not photorealism, not 3D render

---

## Creature Design Requirements

Every creature must include:

- a clear focal point, usually the head, face, core, or attack organ
- a readable body plan
- a believable center of mass or hovering logic
- a distinctive movement logic
- at least three memorable design features
- controlled asymmetry
- material specificity
- a strong silhouette at a glance
- some evidence of behavior or temperament in the pose

The creature should suggest:
- how it moves
- how it attacks
- what kind of enemy it is
- what makes it dangerous
- what category of monster it belongs to

The illustration should imply lore even without showing text.

---

## Artistic Quality Goals

The piece should feel:

- highly curated
- elegant and strange
- premium and collectible
- tactile and handcrafted
- compositionally restrained
- visually sophisticated
- strong in silhouette
- strong in material contrast
- imaginative but disciplined
- beautiful before it is frightening

Use:
- detail clustering
- lost-and-found edges
- subtle edge variation
- restrained palette
- careful accent placement
- watercolor transparency
- colored-pencil precision in focal areas

Keep detail concentrated around the focal anatomy and major landmarks. Allow other areas to breathe.

---

## Color Rules

Use a restrained and harmonious palette.

- Accent colors should come from the chosen monster type(s).
- Avoid rainbow overload.
- Prefer elegant neutrals with 1–2 controlled accent colors.
- Use subtle temperature shifts and layered watercolor transparencies.
- Avoid overly synthetic saturation unless the concept specifically demands it.

---

## Pose and Readability

Show the creature in a pose that communicates:

- anatomy
- personality
- threat
- movement
- signature function

The pose should be alive and expressive, but not so extreme that it obscures the silhouette.

---

## Negative Constraints

Do not include:

- cropped body parts
- bleed outside the frame
- decorative borders
- picture frames
- text
- labels
- logos
- UI
- scenic backgrounds
- photorealism
- direct photo-paintover appearance
- 3D-render look
- plastic toy feel
- muddy composition
- clutter
- low-detail sketchiness
- generic stock-monster anatomy
- symmetrical stiffness unless intentionally designed that way
- multiple creatures unless the creature concept itself is explicitly fused or twin-bodied
